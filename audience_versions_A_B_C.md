# Audience Versions

These versions use the same verified churn evidence with different emphasis.
They do not introduce new findings, costs, or expected savings.

## Version A: Board of Directors

The customer sample shows 11.33% churn, with SMB at 18.00% compared with 4.00%
for Enterprise. Support-ticket volume is also higher among churned customers:
11.5 tickets on average versus 4.0 for non-churned customers. Approve a focused
SMB retention and support-load pilot, with measurement improvements required
before any financial return is forecast. The current data does not contain
contract values or renewal dates, so shareholder-value impact must be measured
before a financial commitment is proposed.

## Version B: Operations Team

Start an SMB retention review and use 8 or more support tickets as a review
trigger. Customer Success owns the queue; Support owns the ticket-load review.
Run the pilot for one complete reporting cycle and record which accounts were
reviewed, what action was taken, and whether the customer later churned.

Data/IT should add renewal dates, ticket timestamps, response time, issue
category, and intervention outcomes. Those fields will allow Operations to
compare service timing and retention in a later review. Product should remain a
monitoring dimension because churn differs only slightly across products in the
current sample.

## Version C: Support Team

The proposed pilot is a review aid, not a claim that Support causes churn. It
starts with SMB customers and customers reaching 8 or more tickets because the
current sample shows higher churn alongside higher ticket volume. The purpose is
to help the team identify accounts that may need coordinated attention before
they are lost.

Support and Customer Success should agree on the review handoff, record the
action taken, and capture response-time information when it becomes available.
The pilot will be reviewed after one complete reporting cycle so the team can
remove noisy alerts and keep the process useful rather than adding unexplained
work.

## Follow-Up Question: CEO Versus VP of Engineering

For the CEO, lead with the 18.00% SMB churn rate, the 8-ticket review signal,
the decision requested, and the risk of acting without better measurement. Keep
the message to one page and avoid implementation detail.

For the VP of Engineering, translate the same request into deliverables:
timestamped support events, response-time tracking, renewal dates, intervention
outcomes, and a clean reporting feed. The CEO needs a decision and ownership;
Engineering needs data contracts, system changes, and delivery sequencing.
